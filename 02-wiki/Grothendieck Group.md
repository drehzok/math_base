# Grothendieck Group

The **Grothendieck group** is a fundamental construction in algebra and topology that associates an abelian group to a category, capturing its "additive" structure.

## Definition

### For Abelian Categories
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an abelian category. The Grothendieck group $K_0(\mathcal{A})$ is the abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes $[X]$ of objects $X \in \mathcal{A}$, subject to the relations:
$[X] = [A] + [B]$
whenever there exists a [[Short Exact Sequence]] $0 \to A \to X \to B \to 0$ in $\mathcal{A}$.

### Split Grothendieck Group
For an additive category $\mathcal{C}$, the **split Grothendieck group** $K_0^{\text{split}}(\mathcal{C})$ is generated by isomorphism classes $[X]$ with relations $[X \oplus Y] = [X] + [Y]$.

## Properties

### Induced Homomorphisms (Lemma 1.1)
If $F: \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$ is an exact functor between abelian categories, it induces a group homomorphism $[F]: K_0(\mathcal{A}) \to K_0(\mathcal{B})$ defined by $[F]([M]) = [F(M)]$.

### Jordan-Hölder Theorem (Lemma 1.2)
In a length category (where every object has a finite filtration with simple quotients), $K_0(\mathcal{A})$ is a free abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes of [[Simple Object]]s $\{L_i\}_{i \in I}$:
$$K_0(\mathcal{A}) \cong \bigoplus_{i \in I} \mathbb{Z} [L_i]$$
The class of any object $X$ is given by $[X] = \sum_{i \in I} [X : L_i] [L_i]$, where $[X : L_i]$ is the multiplicity of $L_i$ in a composition series of $X$.

### Graded Version
If $\mathcal{A}$ is a graded category (e.g., graded modules over a graded algebra), the Grothendieck group becomes a module over the ring of Laurent polynomials $\mathbb{Z}[q, q^{-1}]$. The action of $q$ corresponds to the grading shift operator $\{1\}$.
$$[M\{i\}] = q^i [M]$$

## Examples
- **Finite Dimensional Vector Spaces**: $K_0(\text{fd-Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, where $[V] = \dim(V)$.
- **$R = \mathbb{C}[x]/(x^2)$**: For $R$-mod (fd), there is only one simple object $\mathbb{C}$. Thus $K_0(R\text{-mod}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$. Since there is a non-split sequence $0 \to \mathbb{C} \to R \to \mathbb{C} \to 0$, we have $[R] = [\mathbb{C}] + [\mathbb{C}] = 2[\mathbb{C}]$.
- **Finite Dimensional Modules**: For a finite-dimensional algebra $A$, $K_0(A\text{-mod}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^n$, where $n$ is the number of simple $A$-modules.
